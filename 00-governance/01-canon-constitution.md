# Canon Constitution

1. **One canonical owner.** Every authoritative canon object has exactly one Source-of-Truth owner.
2. **Existence is not authority.** Repository presence does not make material canon.
3. **Canon IDs are permanent.** Never recycle an ID, including rejected or superseded IDs.
4. **No silent changes.** Work on one owner cannot quietly redesign another owner.
5. **Locked means deliberate.** `LOCKED_CANON` changes require explicit canon-change review.
6. **Contradictions block promotion.** Do not guess between conflicting authoritative sources.
7. **Newer is not automatically stronger.** Authority is governance + ownership + status, not timestamps.
8. **Archive preserves history, not authority.** Archived material cannot silently re-enter current canon.
9. **Evidence before claims.** `PASS`, `VERIFIED`, `LOCKED_CANON`, and `PRODUCTION_READY` require evidence.
10. **Builder is not sole grader.** Major changes require an independent review step.
11. **No goalpost shifting.** Review against approved requirements unless governance itself deliberately changes.
12. **Governance cannot casually rewrite itself.** Material governance changes are Class E changes.
13. **Scope is explicit.** Facts may be scoped to a storyworld, universe, continuity, timeline, realm, story, or installment.
14. **Variants do not overwrite originals.** Alternate-universe or alternate-timeline versions get their own Canon IDs.
15. **Decision Records authorize change; they do not become lore owners.** Until affected Source-of-Truth records are migrated, promotion is blocked.
16. **The Canon Registry is the routing index, not a second lore bible.** It locates owners and records status/dependencies; factual prose lives in the owner document.
17. **Unknown stays unknown.** Blank, open, disputed, or intentionally unresolved fields are not permission to invent approved canon.
18. **Template material is not story canon.** Files under `templates/` are framework assets and must never be interpreted as fictional facts.
19. **Cross-scope dependencies are explicit.** A Canon object cannot silently depend on canon that is true only outside its scope; intentional bridges must be declared and reviewed.
