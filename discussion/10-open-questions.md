# Open questions — decision queue, not implementation backlog

## Fundamental laws

- What is an element? Natural force, language category, independent energy, discovered discipline or a combination? Can the same outcome arise from independent affinity and fusion without making either redundant?
- What exactly is a seal: written pattern, physical object, reusable inscription, energy channel or reservoir? Distinguish the inscription from the medium. Who writes it, who can activate it, what can it do, and what prevents unlimited power?
- What is origami made of? Why do different forms have different base properties? Is a figure already special before a seal is applied? Does paper quality, fold skill, pattern, material, size, craftsmanship or form matter? What counts as a physical figure versus a manifestation?
- Is awakening life, consciousness, animation, temporary magic or several different cases? Can an unbound companion retain memory or personality? What is death for a bound origami, and how permanent is it? What is the cost of replacement or recovery?
- Does a single-use thrown talisman generate its own physical form or require a prepared figure? Are the seal, talisman and consumed ink the same object or separate things? Why choose a consumable over a persistent companion?
- How do ancient language, inscription depth, generations of refinement and individual maker skill interact? Does each rewriting add actual power, knowledge, quality, history or some combination? Avoid unlimited stacking and arbitrary magic.

## Ink and professions

- Is ink literally world currency or a standardized commodity exchange medium? Is there mundane ink versus magical ink? Why is it precious and difficult to handle? What happens if the same material is spent in battle and used as money?
- How is ink obtained, prepared, controlled, stored, transported and recovered? What creates scarcity? Who can trade or certify it? Does the player need to participate, or can these be background laws?
- What training qualifies an Ink Master? Is Ink Control a profession independent of combat affinity? How do Ink Masters relate to seal makers, origami makers and wielders? Can professions overlap? Keep Ink Wielder distinct.
- Is battle energy separate from physical ink? If yes, choose terminology after mechanics. If no, explain how ink transforms without undermining currency consistency.
- Are seal creation, trade, buying, selling, collecting, crafting and inheritance required, optional or mostly narrative? What complexity fits the game?

## Wielders and power

- Who can use seals? Access, affinity, mastery and specialization must not be confused. Does everyone start with five? Is personal multi-element proficiency necessary or can teammates contribute? Can an unaligned person use a seal?
- How do figure, seal, wielder, temporary energy and synergy contribute to different stats? No numerical formula selected. What are caps, tradeoffs and counterplay? Avoid making any contributor redundant.
- Which permanent skill paths are meaningful? Are skill points per element or shared? Can players specialize in two/three/all five, and what prevents one universal optimal build? Can they respec?
- What explains upgrades during waves? Combine absorption, saturation and concentration; decide source, capacity, conversion, allocation, dissipation. Do kills produce energy or ink or both? How do peaceful/civilian users get power?
- What persists after battle: learned inscriptions, maker quality, base figure, companion experience, wielder XP, seal improvements, active form, leftover ink? What resets and why? Distinguish wave transition from entire battle ending.
- Retrieval: can the seal or figure be picked up, moved or unbound? During battle or between waves? Cost/cooldown? What happens to invested energy, active upgrades and companions? Salvage versus relocation versus death.

## Reactions, resonance, fusion

- What should remain of existing enemy-status reactions? Different-element hits on one target, cooldown, consumption and effects. No prototype selected as final behavior.
- Are nearby seal links mechanically meaningful, visual hints, support buffs or prerequisites for deliberate fusions? What does range represent in-world? Avoid a universal proximity threshold before rules.
- How does a single fused figure differ from a normal figure with two seals, a linked attack or a newly introduced independent element?
- For two-position and three-position combinations, is adjacency sufficient, or are recipes, energy, mastery, compatible figures, orientation or timing needed? Are participating towers preserved, replaced, temporarily disabled, or consumed?
- Do fusion results have independent base stats or inherit inputs? Can fusion break, be retrieved, change form or persist beyond battle? What counters giant formations?
- Are same-element links allowed? Can support seals boost two or more connected seals without becoming an automatic best choice? How do networks avoid unbounded chaining?
- Six locked categories: can they overlap (e.g., summoned guardian or fused support)? Category membership rules and concrete designs still open.
- Future plants, golems, animal summons, water serpents: periodic spawning, lifespan, targeting, battlefield occupancy, collection and permanence undecided. Summoning may be a technique, class or independent element.

## World, story and product scope

- What is a sanctuary or castle? Who lives there? What is defended: people, structures, artifacts, seals, routes or scroll worlds? Why are enemies attacking? Are origami enemies crafted, living, corrupted or simply opposing defenders?
- Who is the player: individual wielder, keeper, commander, collector, community or ensemble? Why do they have all five starting seals?
- How do new Lightning, Blood, Forest or other powers enter through exploration, discovery, conflict or cooperation? How do we make earned trust credible and avoid repetitive "beat them, become friends" plots? Can new powers simply be added for fun without a major story arc?
- No fixed factions, cultures, geography, history, cosmology, cast, moral alignments or permanent opponents. What scope best supports the actual product?
- Is the first public game a traditional tower defense, expanded strategy, companion collection game, or another coherent combination? Which systems belong in lore only versus actual mechanics? No locked complexity tier, development roadmap or new game genre.
- How can the existing prototype evidence guide, but not constrain, our original product?
