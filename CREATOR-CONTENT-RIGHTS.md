## Different kinds of files have different rights

**Reusable Writing Universe Template framework files** (blank templates, Canon governance, structural schemas and supplied guides) are licensed under their own **Writing Universe Template Proprietary Use License 1.1** in `LICENSE`. Keep that license and its notices with those files. Reusing the framework does **not** transfer ownership of your original fiction to its copyright holder.

**Your original fiction and production files** (new prose, canon, characters, artwork, audio, scripts, private research and storyworld-specific edits) belong to their actual authors or other lawful rights holders, subject to applicable law, contracts, third-party material and platform terms. Merely being in a repo built from the framework does not mean the Framework rights holder owns your fiction.

**Public GitHub repository notice:** GitHub's Terms of Service permit platform viewing and forking of public content and include contribution-under-repository-license provisions. Do not assume that a `LICENSE` file and this guide alone resolve every ownership/licensing question for public or collaborative fictional work. Before making your storyworld public, review GitHub's applicable terms and decide the rights applicable to original content, including outside contributions.

## Before collaborating or going public

1. Confirm the actual rights holders for original creative contributions. Do not add third-party art, fiction, code or research without appropriate permission.
2. Decide whether others may copy, remix, redistribute, commercially use, or only view original storyworld material. **Default project intent is no additional grant** beyond permissions necessarily provided by law and the hosting service; if your intention differs, expressly document it with valid terms and contributor permissions.
3. Decide how collaboration works: who owns contributed material, what contributors license, and who can publish or modify original content. Do not infer all collaborator work is automatically owned by the repository account.
4. Maintain framework/canon separation: the proprietary framework license governs framework assets; any original-content rights notice is a separate author-controlled decision.
5. Keep the repository **private** while permissions, ownership and intended publication terms are unsettled.

**Template maintainers cannot approve your ownership or publication choices for you.** Legal review may be important before commercial or large-scale public projects.
